title: How Much Should You Budget for Home Maintenance Each Year?
date: 2026-11-24
category: Homeownership
summary: A common rule is to set aside 1% to 2% of your home's value each year for upkeep. Here is how to size the number for your own home.
---
Most sources suggest setting aside about 1% to 2% of your home's value every year for maintenance and repairs, and some go as high as 4% for older homes. On a $300,000 home, 1% to 2% works out to $3,000 to $6,000 a year, or $250 to $500 a month.

No single number fits every house, so here is how the common rules work and how to pick the one that matches your home.

## What are the common rules of thumb?

Three rules come up most often:

- **The percent-of-value rule.** Zillow recommends setting aside 1% to 2% of what your home is worth each year; its example is a $364,000 home, which comes to $3,640 to $7,280 a year. Redfin and NerdWallet both cite a wider range of 1% to 4%, and NerdWallet's example of a $540,000 home runs from $5,400 at 1% to $21,600 at 4%.
- **The square-foot rule.** Redfin says you can save about $1 per square foot each year. For a 3,000-square-foot home, that is about $3,000 a year.
- **The monthly cushion.** If those totals feel like a stretch, Redfin notes that even $50 to $100 a month builds a cushion.

The rules overlap but do not match, and that is the point: they are starting places, not guarantees.

## How much do homeowners actually spend?

It depends on what you count. A NerdWallet article citing the Census Bureau's 2019 American Housing Survey says the average homeowner spent $950 on maintenance and repairs, about 0.6% of home value. That survey also found spending was lower for newer homes (0.2% for homes built in the 2010s) and higher for older ones (0.8% for homes built before 1960).

More recent figures count hired professionals. Thumbtack's Q2 2026 report puts average yearly spending on single-family homes at $11,196, up 2% from a year earlier. Zillow's research with Thumbtack, published in 2025, put the national figure at $10,867. These are costs of work done by pros on that platform, so they will run higher than a survey that includes do-it-yourself jobs. Zillow also found the cost varies by metro area, from $9,132 in Dallas to $12,921 in Seattle.

The takeaway from the gap between those numbers: the percent rules are a safety net for the years when something big breaks, while the averages tell you what a typical year can look like.

## What should push your number higher or lower?

Start at 1% and adjust for the facts of your house:

- **Age.** Older homes tend to need more. The Census figures above show the pattern.
- **Size.** More square footage means more roof, more paint, and more to heat and cool.
- **Climate and location.** NerdWallet notes that a laminated-shingle roof can last 35 to 40 years in St. Louis but under 15 years in Florida. Heat, humidity, and storms shorten the life of many parts of a home.
- **What you have already replaced.** A new roof or recent HVAC system lowers your near-term risk. Original systems on an older home raise it.
- **Condo or HOA living.** Thumbtack's figures show condos averaging $3,532 a year and townhomes $9,354, versus $11,196 for single-family homes. Check what your association covers.

## What do common jobs cost?

These figures are from Redfin and Zillow, and your prices will vary:

- HVAC servicing: about $150 to $300 per visit, once or twice a year (Redfin).
- Water heater maintenance: about $100 a year, with replacement from $800 to $1,500 (Redfin).
- Furnace: maintenance of $100 to $300 a year, with replacement from $2,500 to $7,500 (Redfin).
- Gutter cleaning: about $278 on average (Zillow).
- Basic roof repairs: $200 to $500, with extensive damage reaching into the thousands (Redfin).

NerdWallet also suggests planning ahead for big systems. Its example: a central air conditioner typically lasts 15 to 20 years, so a $4,000 system means setting aside about $400 a year to replace it.

## How do I make this easy?

Open a separate savings account for the house and move a fixed amount into it every month, the same way you pay the mortgage. Then:

1. Pick your starting percentage from the rules above.
2. Divide by 12 and set up an automatic transfer.
3. List your big systems, with their ages, and note when each is likely to need replacing.
4. Review the account once a year and adjust.

If you are buying, ask your agent and inspector about the age and condition of the roof, HVAC, and water heater, and fold that into your budget before you make an offer.

## The takeaway

Start with 1% to 2% of your home's value each year, move toward the higher end if the home is older or its major systems are original, and save it monthly so a repair is an inconvenience instead of an emergency.
