---
title: What Do October's Home Sales Numbers Mean for Buyers and Sellers?
date: 2026-11-19
category: Market Updates
summary: Existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October while prices held steady and supply grew. Here is what that means for buyers and sellers.
---
Nationally, October was a slower month: sales dropped, inventory grew, and prices barely moved. For buyers that means more choices and a little more room to negotiate. For sellers it means pricing and preparation matter more than they did a year ago.

Here is the data behind that, and how we read it. This is general market information, not advice for your specific situation.

## What happened to home sales in October?

The National Association of Realtors reported that [existing-home sales](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/existing-home-sales-decreased-5-5-percent-october-nar-report) came in at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.75 million in October. That is down 5.5% from September and down 8.8% from a year earlier.

The national median existing-home price was $416,700, up just 0.4% from $414,900 in October 2025. In other words, prices are roughly flat while the number of sales is lower.

## Is there more inventory for buyers?

Yes. NAR reported 1.59 million homes for sale at the end of October, up 4.6% from a year earlier. That equals 5.1 months of supply, compared with 4.4 months in October 2025. Homes also took a little longer to sell, with a median of 35 days on the market versus 34 a year ago.

A balanced market is often described as around five to six months of supply, so national conditions are close to that middle ground. Local markets can look quite different from the national picture, including here in Greater Baton Rouge, the Gulf Coast of Alabama, Greater Huntsville, and the Northshore.

## What are mortgage rates doing?

Rates are the main reason buyers have been cautious. NAR noted that the 30-year fixed rate averaged 7.33% in October, according to Freddie Mac, compared with 6.25% a year earlier. In Freddie Mac's [weekly survey dated November 12, 2026](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms), the 30-year fixed averaged 7.11% and the 15-year fixed averaged 6.45%. A year earlier those figures were 6.24% and 5.49%.

Rates have eased from October's average, but they remain higher than a year ago. Rates change weekly, so check with a lender for what you would actually qualify for.

## Are first-time buyers still buying?

They are, but in smaller numbers. First-time buyers made up 29% of October sales, down from 31% in September and 32% a year earlier.

## What does this mean if you are buying?

- **You have more options.** More listings and slightly longer market times usually mean less pressure to decide in a day.
- **Run the monthly payment, not just the price.** A higher rate changes the payment on the same price. Ask a lender to show you a few scenarios.
- **Negotiation is more realistic.** Sellers may be open to repairs, credits, or flexibility on timing. Your agent can tell you what is happening in your specific neighborhood and price range.
- **Do not wait for a perfect moment.** Nobody can predict rates. Buy when the home and the payment work for your life.

## What does this mean if you are selling?

- **Price it right from day one.** With more competition, an overpriced home tends to sit. Your agent should walk you through recent comparable sales.
- **Presentation matters.** Clean, decluttered, well-maintained homes stand out when buyers have choices.
- **Expect a slower pace than a year ago.** A median of 35 days nationally is a reminder to plan your timeline with some cushion.
- **Ask for a net sheet.** Know what you would walk away with after costs before you decide.

## The takeaway

The national numbers point to a market that is cooler but not broken: flat prices, more inventory, and rates that are higher than last year but off their October average. The best move is a local one. Talk with an agent who can show you the numbers for your street and price point, and with a lender about your options, then decide from there.

Consult a mortgage professional, tax advisor, or attorney for advice about your own finances.
