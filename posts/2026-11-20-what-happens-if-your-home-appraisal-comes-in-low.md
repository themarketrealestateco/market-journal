title: What Happens If Your Home Appraisal Comes In Lower Than the Offer Price?

summary: A low appraisal doesn't have to end the deal. You can challenge errors, renegotiate, bring more cash, or walk away if your contract allows.

If the appraisal comes in below your offer price, the deal is not automatically dead. The lender will base your loan on the lower value, so you generally have four paths: ask for a review if the report has errors, renegotiate the price with the seller, cover the difference with more cash, or cancel under an appraisal contingency if your contract includes one.

Here is how each path works, and how to decide which one fits.

## What is an appraisal, and who pays for it?

An appraisal is a third-party opinion of a home's market value, prepared by a professional with state-issued appraiser credentials. [Freddie Mac](https://myhome.freddiemac.com/blog/homebuying/what-homebuyers-can-expect-appraisal-and-what-do-if-its-below-your-offer-price) explains that its main job is to help your lender judge the collateral, meaning the home that secures the loan. It also gives you a check on whether the agreed price is reasonable.

The appraiser looks at things like size, age, room count, condition, location, lot size, upgrades, and recent sales of comparable homes. The lender orders the appraisal, and you will likely reimburse the lender, usually as part of closing costs. Freddie Mac gives a median cost of $450 to $700, varying by region. Expect about one to two weeks from the first visit to the final report, and longer when appraisal activity is high.

## Why does a low appraisal matter?

Your lender will only lend against the appraised value, not the price you agreed to pay. Say the contract price is higher than the appraised value. The loan amount is based on the lower figure, and, as Freddie Mac notes, the buyer would have to pay the difference up front if the price doesn't change.

## What are my options if the appraisal is low?

**1. Check the report for errors and ask for a review.** Read the appraisal closely. Look for missing or inaccurate details, such as the wrong square footage, overlooked upgrades, or comparable sales that don't fit your home. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes a ["reconsideration of value"](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/mortgage-borrowers-can-challenge-inaccurate-appraisals-through-the-reconsideration-of-value-process), which lets a borrower ask the lender to take another look. The CFPB lists these grounds: factual errors or omissions, inadequate comparable properties, or evidence that the appraisal was influenced by prohibited bias. Lenders typically explain how to raise a concern, so ask your loan officer what their process is. Freddie Mac adds that the lender makes the formal request, and generally only when the original report is judged to be inaccurate.

**2. Renegotiate the price.** Share the appraisal with the seller and use its comparable sales to support a lower price. A seller who understands that the next buyer's lender will face the same appraisal may be open to meeting in the middle.

**3. Bring more money to closing.** If the seller won't budge and you still want the home, you can cover the gap between the appraised value and the contract price in cash, in addition to your down payment. Only do this if the numbers work for your budget and you are comfortable with what the home is worth to you.

**4. Walk away.** If your contract has an appraisal contingency, it should allow you to cancel. This is why contingencies matter, and why it pays to understand yours before you sign. Waiving one can leave you responsible for the gap.

## How can I prepare before the appraisal?

Some of this is within your control before the appraiser ever visits.

- **Read your contract's appraisal language** with your agent on day one, not after the report arrives.
- **Know the comparable sales.** Ask your agent for recent sales of similar homes so the price doesn't surprise you.
- **Have a backup plan and a cash cushion** in case the value comes in short.
- **Ask your loan officer about alternatives.** Freddie Mac notes that some loans qualify for an automated appraisal waiver that can replace the in-person appraisal, so ask whether you do.
- **Give the appraiser what helps.** A list of recent upgrades and improvements, with dates, can help the appraiser account for them.

My team has been through just about every kind of market, and one thing holds up in all of them: the buyers who stay calm and look at the facts of the report make the best decisions.

## The takeaway

A low appraisal is a problem to solve, not an automatic end to the purchase. Read the report line by line, ask about a reconsideration if you find errors, and use the comparable sales to negotiate. Decide ahead of time how much of a gap you could comfortably cover, if any, and make sure your contract's appraisal contingency fits your plan.
