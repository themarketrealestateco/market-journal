---
title: Do You Need Flood Insurance When You Buy a Home?
date: 2026-11-27
category: Homeownership
summary: Most homeowners policies do not cover flood damage. Here is when flood insurance is required, what it covers, and why to ask before you make an offer.
---
Most standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage, so if you want protection from rising water you generally need a separate flood policy. Whether you are required to buy one depends on your mortgage and where the home sits, and in places like Baton Rouge, the rest of Louisiana, and the Gulf Coast of Alabama, it is worth asking about before you make an offer.

Here is a common scene: the inspection is done, the appraisal is in, and then someone says the words "flood zone" two weeks before closing. A few questions up front keep that from becoming a surprise.

## Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Usually not. FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) page says most homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage, and the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) says flood is not typically covered by most homeowners and renters policies. Flood is its own policy, sold through the NFIP or through private insurers. Read your own policy and ask your insurance professional what is and is not included.

## When is flood insurance required?

It is required when two things are true: the home is in a high-risk flood area, and your mortgage comes from a government-backed or federally regulated lender. NAIC defines a high-risk area as one with at least a 1% annual chance of flooding. NAIC also notes that some lenders require flood insurance even outside high-risk areas, so the requirement can come from your loan terms and not only from a map.

If you are paying cash, a lender is not there to require it, but the flood risk is the same.

## Can a home outside a high-risk zone still flood?

Yes. FloodSmart's guide for insurance agents says almost one-third of NFIP claims from 2014 to 2024 came from areas outside high-risk zones, and that a property does not have to be near water to flood. A recent Government Accountability Office report (October 2026) found about 43% of properties in the highest-risk areas had flood insurance versus 2% outside them, so many homeowners are carrying that risk without a policy.

A flood zone label is a starting point, not a guarantee in either direction.

## What does an NFIP flood policy cover?

For a single-family home, FloodSmart says NFIP policies cover up to $250,000 for the structure and up to $100,000 for belongings inside, with contents coverage bought separately. If your home is worth more than that, ask about private flood insurance or extra coverage. NAIC says NFIP coverage is typically around $700 a year in high-risk areas, but your price depends on the specific property, so get a quote for the home you are actually buying.

## When does the policy start?

FloodSmart says coverage typically takes effect 30 days after you buy, with exceptions. There is no waiting period when the policy is bought as part of a mortgage that is being made, increased, extended, or renewed. NAIC says the same for a policy bought when you buy a home. If you are buying without a mortgage, do not wait until closing week, because a flood policy bought late may not be in force when you need it.

## What should you do before you make an offer?

Work through these in order:

1. **Look up the property's flood zone** and ask your agent to find out whether the seller has an elevation certificate or a past flood insurance policy.
2. **Get a flood insurance quote early**, ideally during your inspection period, so the premium is part of your monthly budget and not a closing-week surprise. Our post on [how much closing costs are and who pays them](/posts/how-much-are-closing-costs-for-buyers/) covers the other costs worth planning for.
3. **Ask the seller about any history of water in the home or yard**, and read the inspection report with that in mind. If something turns up, our post on [what you can ask for after a home inspection](/posts/what-to-ask-for-after-a-home-inspection/) explains your options.
4. **Ask your insurance professional about private flood coverage** if the NFIP limits may not be enough for the home.

## The takeaway

Assume your homeowners policy does not cover flood until your insurance professional tells you otherwise, get a flood insurance quote before your inspection period ends, and do it even if the home is outside a mapped high-risk zone. The cost of asking is small compared with finding out after water has come in.
