Becoming a real estate agent takes education, a licensing exam, and a broker to work under. In Louisiana the pre-licensing requirement is 90 hours. In Alabama it is 60 hours, followed by a 30-hour post-license course. Rules differ by state, so always confirm the current details with your state's real estate commission before you enroll.

Our team is licensed in both states, so here is how each path works.

## What does it take to get licensed in Louisiana?

According to the [Louisiana Real Estate Commission](https://lrec.gov/become-licensed/salesperson/), a salesperson applicant must:

- Be at least 18 years old and have a high school diploma or GED.
- Complete 90 hours of real estate education in courses approved by the commission.
- Pass the real estate salesperson licensing examination.
- Be supervised by a licensed real estate broker and show proof of errors and omissions insurance before the license is issued.
- Follow the commission's application procedures.

The [commission's pre-licensing page](https://lrec.gov/become-licensed/salesperson/pre-licensing-education) says the 90 hours cover real estate principles and practices, Louisiana license law, commission rules, the law of agency, and civil law as it relates to real estate. The 30-hour portion on license law, rules, agency and civil law cannot be replaced with out-of-state credit. Courses must come from a state-certified real estate school.

After you are licensed, the same page says post-licensing education is due within 180 days of your initial license date. New licensees also complete 12 hours of continuing education in their first licensed year before renewing an active license.

## What does it take to get licensed in Alabama?

The [Alabama Real Estate Commission](https://arec.alabama.gov/docs/license%20law%20changes%202025_steps%20to%20become%20licensed%20Salesperson_080625.pdf) lays out these steps for salespeople under the current rules:

1. Complete a 60-hour salesperson pre-license course.
2. Pass the salesperson license examination.
3. Apply for a temporary salesperson license. It can be issued as active or inactive, and you must be licensed under a company to be active.
4. Complete a 30-hour post-license course.
5. Apply for an original salesperson license to replace the temporary one.

Each step has its own time limit, so check the commission's document for the deadlines. The same document says the process changes after October 1, 2028, adding a second 60-hour course, a 15-hour orientation in a specific area of real estate, and a core competencies checklist signed by the qualifying broker. If you plan to start around then, check which version applies to you.

## Why does the broker you choose matter so much?

The license is only the starting line. In both states you work under a broker, and that choice shapes what you learn in your first years. A few questions are worth asking before you hang your license anywhere:

- What training is offered after the post-license course, and how often?
- How are leads handled, and are there costs beyond your own marketing?
- Who answers your questions when a contract or negotiation gets complicated?
- What are the fee structures, and is everything in writing?

In our experience, the answers to these questions tell you more about a brokerage than its marketing does.

## What should you do before you enroll?

Take a realistic look at the first year. Income in this business is commission-based, so plan for the time between getting licensed and your first closing. Talk to a few working agents about how they spent their first six months. A tax or financial professional can help you plan for self-employment costs, which we are not able to advise on here.

**The takeaway:** Check your state commission's current requirements, pick an approved school, and interview more than one broker before you decide where to hang your license. The steps are clear, and the broker you choose will shape your first years more than the course will.
