Buyers usually pay between 2% and 5% of the purchase price in closing costs, on top of the down payment. [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/learn/closing-costs/) and [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/blog/what-are-closing-costs/) both give that range, and both use the same example: on a $300,000 home, that is roughly $6,000 to $15,000.

Your exact number depends on your loan type, your lender, your location, and the home itself. Here is what goes into it, how to get a real estimate, and what you can do to bring it down.

## What are closing costs?

Closing costs are the fees and prepaid items due when your purchase closes. They are separate from your down payment. Redfin notes that the down payment is paid at closing but listed separately from closing costs.

Common categories include:

- **Lender fees:** application, origination, underwriting or processing, and credit reporting.
- **Third-party services:** appraisal, home inspection, survey, and flood certification.
- **Title and settlement:** title search, lender's and owner's title insurance, closing or settlement fees, and recording fees.
- **Prepaids:** homeowners insurance, property taxes, and prepaid interest, which are often collected at closing.
- **Optional or loan-specific items:** discount points, HOA transfer fees, and mortgage insurance charges that depend on your loan program.

Some items, such as the inspection or appraisal, are often paid before closing day, but they still count toward what it costs to buy.

## How do I find out my actual number?

Do not rely on a rule of thumb for your final budget. Zillow advises that the best way to estimate your costs is to review the Loan Estimate your lender provides. It lists the expected fees for your specific loan.

Before closing, you will also receive a Closing Disclosure from your lender. Zillow says it arrives at least three business days before closing, and recommends comparing it line by line with your Loan Estimate. Ask your lender about anything that changed. Redfin cautions that costs can shift at the last minute, so give yourself time to review.

Your earnest money deposit is not an extra cost. Redfin explains it is credited toward your total cash to close.

## Can the seller pay my closing costs?

Sometimes. You can ask the seller to cover some or all of your closing costs as part of your offer. These are called seller concessions. The seller can agree, counter, or decline, and how much a seller is allowed to contribute depends on your loan.

Zillow lists these limits, which can vary and should be confirmed with your lender:

- **Conventional loans:** up to 3%, 6%, or 9% of the loan amount, depending on your down payment.
- **FHA loans:** up to 6% of the lower of the appraised value or purchase price.
- **VA loans:** up to 4% of the loan amount.
- **USDA loans:** up to 6% of the sale price.

In Zillow's 2025 Consumer Housing Trends Report, buyers said sellers agreed to pay some (23%) or all (44%) of their closing costs. Every deal is different, and what is realistic depends on your market and the competing offers.

## How can I lower what I pay?

There is no way to avoid closing costs entirely, but you have options:

- **Compare lenders.** Fees vary, so ask for Loan Estimates from more than one lender and compare the fees, not just the rate.
- **Negotiate.** Ask the seller for a contribution, and ask your lender about fees that look high.
- **Think carefully about discount points.** Paying points raises your cash needed at closing, so make sure the math works for how long you plan to stay.
- **Look into assistance.** Some down payment and closing cost assistance programs exist. Ask a lender what is available where you are buying.

You can sometimes roll costs into the loan, but Zillow notes you will pay more interest over time.

My team has been through just about every kind of closing table, and the buyers who are never surprised are the ones who asked about cash to close early, before they wrote an offer.

## The takeaway

Plan for 2% to 5% of the purchase price on top of your down payment, then replace that estimate with real numbers from your Loan Estimate as soon as you have one. Ask your agent and your lender to walk you through each line before closing day.
