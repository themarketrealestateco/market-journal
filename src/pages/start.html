Buying a home: 8 steps

A plain-English guide for buyers in Greater Baton Rouge, the Northshore, Greater Huntsville and the Alabama Gulf Coast.

  1. Get pre-approved first. A lender reviews your income, credit and savings and tells you what you can borrow. Sellers take offers with a pre-approval letter far more seriously.
  2. Set your real monthly number. Add principal, interest, property tax, homeowners insurance and, where it applies, flood insurance and HOA dues. Get insurance quotes before you fall in love with a house.
  3. Choose your agent. Your agent searches, schedules showings, pulls comparable sales and negotiates for you.
  4. Tour and shortlist. Check the flood zone, roof age, drainage and drive times on every home you like.
  5. Make an offer. In Louisiana, offers are written on the Louisiana Residential Agreement to Buy or Sell. Your deposit is held in escrow under Louisiana Real Estate Commission rules.
  6. Inspect and appraise. Order inspections right away, including termite. Your lender orders the appraisal.
  7. Clear title and finalize your loan. Your lender and title company confirm the home can legally transfer to you and that your loan is ready.
  8. Close. Louisiana closes with an act of sale signed before a notary, and the buyer chooses the notary or settlement agent. Alabama closings are typically handled by a closing attorney. You receive your Closing Disclosure at least 3 business days before closing, so review every line.

What it costs

2% to 6%

Buyer closing costs commonly run about 2% to 6% of the price, depending on your loan program and what you negotiate. Your lender's Loan Estimate gives your real number, so ask for one early.

  • Lender and title: Loan fees, appraisal, title search and title insurance, recording and notary fees.
  • Inspections: General inspection, plus termite and any specialty inspection your agent recommends.
  • Prepaids: The first year of homeowners insurance and a few months of taxes and insurance set aside in escrow.
  • Flood insurance: Not part of a standard homeowners policy. Ask your agent to check the flood zone and get a quote early, because FEMA prices flood coverage by each property's own risk.
  • Property taxes: Louisiana's homestead exemption lowers parish property tax on your primary residence. Apply with your parish assessor after you close.
  • Transfer taxes: Louisiana has no state transfer tax. Alabama charges a deed tax and a mortgage recording tax.

Who pays what is negotiable. In the Louisiana contract, act of sale costs and financing costs fall to the buyer unless you negotiate otherwise, and taxes are prorated at the act of sale.

Selling your home: 7 steps

Start with the right price. A plain-English guide for sellers in Louisiana and Alabama.

  1. Price it from the market. Your agent pulls recent comparable sales, active competition and days on market to set a price that attracts offers in the first two weeks.
  2. Prepare the home. Declutter, fix the small things, and get professional photos. First impressions online decide who books a showing.
  3. Complete your disclosures. Louisiana sellers complete the Property Disclosure Document before the buyer commits. Details are below.
  4. List and show. Your listing goes on the MLS and out to the major portals, with a plan for social media, email and your agent's own network.
  5. Negotiate the offer. Price is only one term. Compare financing type, deposit, inspection period, closing date and requested repairs.
  6. Get through inspection and appraisal. Expect a repair request. Your agent helps you decide what to fix, credit or decline.
  7. Close. In Louisiana, you sign the act of sale before a notary, taxes are prorated, and you receive your proceeds after closing.

Seller costs and your net sheet

Seller costs usually include the agent commission, which is fully negotiable between you and your agent, plus prorated taxes, title and recording items, and any concessions you agree to. Ask for a written net sheet before you list, so you know your number before an offer arrives.

Disclosures

Louisiana. State law (La. R.S. 9:3196 to 3200) requires sellers of residential property to give buyers a Property Disclosure Document, using the Louisiana Real Estate Commission form or one with the same required information. It covers defects you know about. You are not required to hire an inspector first, and an "as is" sale does not erase the duty to disclose what you know. Some sellers qualify for an exemption, and your agent will walk you through it.

Alabama. Alabama follows "buyer beware," so there is no statutory seller disclosure form and buyers carry the inspection work. Sellers still cannot commit fraud or hide a known health or safety hazard, and they must answer direct questions truthfully. Homes built before 1978 require the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

If you are selling, tell the truth and put it in writing.
